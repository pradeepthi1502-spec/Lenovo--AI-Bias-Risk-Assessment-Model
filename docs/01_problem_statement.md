
## Background

UK universities are increasingly adopting AI tools across student support, assessment, academic integrity, learning analytics, career services, and administrative workflows.

The original version of this project focused on the risk of gender bias in AI systems used in university admissions. That framing was useful as a policy concern, but it was too hypothetical for the current UK higher education context because admissions decisions remain largely human-led.

The project has therefore been reframed around a more immediate and operationally relevant problem.

## Problem Statement

The main near-term AI governance risk in UK universities is not fully automated admissions decision-making.

The more immediate risk is that universities are deploying or piloting AI systems such as:

- AI plagiarism and detection tools
- Student-facing chatbots
- VLE engagement analytics
- Career-service AI tools
- AI-assisted marking and feedback tools
- Early-alert or learning analytics systems

These systems can influence academic suspicion, student support, feedback quality, progression, employability, and access to opportunities.

At the same time, students do not enter university with equal AI access, confidence, or proficiency. Evidence from UK higher education indicates a growing digital divide in generative AI use, with male students, STEM and Health students, and more socioeconomically advantaged students more likely to use AI.

This creates the central project question:

> Are universities deploying AI systems in ways that unintentionally advantage students who are already more AI-proficient and disadvantage students with lower AI access, confidence, or institutional support?

## Project Aim

The aim of this project is to build a responsible AI governance toolkit that helps universities assess whether AI systems create gender-linked fairness risks before those systems are scaled across student populations.

The toolkit combines:

- AI system inventory
- Representation analysis
- Fairness metrics
- Proxy-bias detection
- Governance evidence validation
- Control maturity scoring
- Residual risk calculation
- Deployment decision support
