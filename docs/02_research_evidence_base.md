# Research Evidence Base

This document summarises the evidence base behind the project. The purpose is not to reproduce the full policy framework, but to explain why the toolkit focuses on live and near-future AI governance risks in UK higher education.

## 1. AI Deployment in UK Universities Is More Immediate Outside Admissions

The original project considered AI bias in admissions. However, current UK university deployment is more visible in areas such as chatbots, AI detection tools, VLE analytics, career services, and assessment support.

This matters because a credible governance toolkit should assess systems that universities are actually using or actively piloting.

## 2. Student-Facing Chatbots Are a Confirmed Deployment Area

Jisc’s LearnWise pilot reported that 12 institutions completed a pilot between August and December 2024, including eight further education colleges and four higher education institutions.

These assistants were piloted in student-facing support contexts, including integration with platforms such as Moodle and Canvas.

Project implication:

> Student-facing chatbots should be treated as a live deployment category in the toolkit.

Primary risk areas:

- Inaccurate advice
- Unequal usage by student group
- Failure to escalate safeguarding or wellbeing concerns
- Lack of transparency about AI-generated support
- Weak governance evidence from vendors or institutions

## 3. AI Detection Tools Are a High-Urgency Governance Risk

AI detection tools are especially sensitive because they may influence academic misconduct suspicion and disciplinary processes.

The key governance concern is not only overall accuracy. In academic integrity contexts, false positives are particularly serious because a wrongly flagged student may face reputational, academic, or emotional harm.

Project implication:

> AI detection tools require fairness auditing by subgroup, especially false positive rates, appeal outcomes, and human override patterns.

Recommended audit metrics:

- False positive rate by gender
- False negative rate by gender
- Detection flag rate by gender
- Appeal success rate by gender
- Human override rate by gender
- Misconduct case conversion rate by gender

Important limitation:

> This project should not claim that gendered false-positive harm has already been proven without institutional data. It should present this as a plausible and testable risk.

## 4. AI-Assisted Marking and Feedback Is Moving from Pilot to Practice

Jisc’s AI in Assessment Pilot is designed to explore AI-assisted marking and feedback through tools such as Graide, KEATH, and TeacherMatic.

Project implication:

> AI-assisted marking should be treated as an active pilot or near-future deployment category.

Primary risk areas:

- Unequal feedback quality
- Weak human moderation
- Score drift
- Over-reliance on automated feedback
- Poor explanation quality
- Differential impact by writing style, subject area, or student group


## 5. Student AI Use Is Unequal

HEPI’s 2025 Student Generative AI Survey reports a growing digital divide in AI use, with male students, STEM and Health students, and more socioeconomically advantaged students more likely to use AI.

Project implication:

> Universities should not assume all students benefit equally from AI-integrated systems.

This supports the need for a Gender AI Proficiency Gap Monitor.

Example monitoring indicators:

- AI usage by gender
- AI confidence by gender
- AI training completion by gender
- Tool adoption by subject area
- Assessment outcomes after AI integration
- Academic misconduct flags after AI policy changes
- Career-tool usage by gender

## 6. Policy and Governance Foundation

The toolkit builds on responsible AI governance principles including:

- Risk-based AI assessment
- Human oversight
- Transparency
- Fairness testing
- Data protection impact assessment
- Equality impact assessment
- Student rights and appeal routes
- Monitoring and incident response

These principles align with major governance frameworks such as:

- NIST AI Risk Management Framework
- ICO AI and data protection guidance
- UK GDPR
- Equality Act 2010
- Public Sector Equality Duty
- OECD AI Principles
- EU AI Act
- Russell Group AI principles
